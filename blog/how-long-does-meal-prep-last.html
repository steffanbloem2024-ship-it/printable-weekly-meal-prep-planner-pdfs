How long does Sunday meal prep last? Count the days to Friday

Published 26 November 2026 · 6 min read

You cook on Sunday so the week runs itself. Monday’s chicken is fine. But the rice you cooked for Friday’s burrito bowls has been sitting in the fridge for five days by the time anyone eats it — and most meal-prep plans never mention that.

This is the one piece of arithmetic every weekend prep plan needs, and it takes about ten seconds per dinner. It is also the reason a lot of “prep everything on Sunday” advice quietly goes wrong in the second half of the week.

1. The rule: cooked food keeps three to four days in the fridge

The US food-safety guidance (USDA and FoodSafety.gov) is that cooked leftovers — chicken, rice, pasta, roast vegetables, sauces — keep for three to four days in a fridge at 40°F / 4°C or colder, and should go into the fridge within two hours of cooking. After that, eat it, freeze it or bin it.

Two honest caveats. First, guidance differs by country, and some is stricter: the UK’s NHS, for example, says cooked rice should be cooled quickly and eaten within 24 hours. Follow your own country’s food-safety agency where it says something different. Second, this page is about ordinary family dinners, not medical advice; if someone in your house is pregnant, very young, elderly or immunocompromised, be more cautious, not less.

2. Count the days from prep day, not from Monday

The mistake is counting from the start of the school week. Count from the day you cooked. If you prep on Sunday:

Dinner nightAge of Sunday’s cooked foodWhat to do
Monday1 dayFridge
Tuesday2 daysFridge
Wednesday3 daysFridge
Thursday4 daysFreeze it on Sunday, or cook it midweek
Friday5 daysFreeze it on Sunday, or cook it midweek

Thursday is the judgement call. Four days is inside “three to four”, but it is the very edge, and real fridges are often warmer than 4°C, especially a full one that gets opened twenty times a day. We treat day four as already out. If Sunday was a Saturday, everything moves up a day and Wednesday becomes the edge.

3. Only cooked steps count

Not every prep job ages the same way. Chopping peppers, mixing a spice blend, making a marinade or washing lettuce does not start the three-to-four-day clock in the same way, because you have not cooked anything. What starts it is cooking: roasting the chicken, boiling the rice, making the sauce, baking the potatoes.

So when you write your prep list, put a mark next to every step that cooks food. Those are the only steps that need the day count. That is the whole system.

4. The two fixes for Thursday and Friday

  • Freeze that portion on prep day. Cook the whole batch on Sunday, but put Thursday’s and Friday’s share straight into the freezer once it has cooled. Move it to the fridge the night before to thaw. This keeps the “one cooking session” benefit and costs you one line on Wednesday night: move the rice to the fridge.
  • Cook it midweek instead. For things that are quick anyway — rice, pasta, a pan of mince — doing a fresh batch on Wednesday or Thursday is often less effort than freezing and thawing. Two days later it is still well inside the window.

The trap in both is the same: a prep plan that says “freeze Friday’s half” on Sunday and then never tells you to thaw it. Write the thaw step onto the night before, on paper, where you will see it.

5. Plan the week so it needs fewer fixes

The simplest fix is ordering. Put the dinners that lean on Sunday’s cooking early in the week, and the ones that need nothing cooked ahead — fresh fish, an omelette, tacos with raw toppings, a freezer-to-oven dinner — on Thursday and Friday. A week ordered that way often needs no freezing at all.
