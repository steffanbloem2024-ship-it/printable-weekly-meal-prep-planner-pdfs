What to make for dinner this week

Published 14 November 2026 · 5 min read

If you are searching this, you probably don’t need recipes. You already know how to cook a dozen dinners your family will eat. What you need is for someone to just pick — because deciding, every single night, is the tiring part. So here is a way to pick a whole week in about ten minutes, and two finished weeks you can take as they are.

Plan five dinners, not seven

Almost every meal plan template has seven boxes. Almost no family actually cooks seven planned dinners: there is a late practice, a birthday, leftovers that need eating, a night everyone is too tired. When the plan says seven and life delivers five, the week feels like a failure by Thursday and the plan gets abandoned.

Plan five. Leave two nights open on purpose — for leftovers, takeout, eggs on toast, or the dinner you got invited to. An open night is not a missed night. It is the slack that lets the other five actually happen.

The five-slot mix that makes picking easy

A blank week is hard to fill because every option competes with every other option. Give each night a job and the choice shrinks to two or three candidates:

  1. An oven night. Everything on one tray, 30–40 minutes, and you are free while it cooks. Good for the evening you get home earliest. e.g. sheet-pan chicken thighs, potatoes and green beans.
  2. A fast night. On the table in 20 minutes or less, for your worst evening of the week. e.g. beef and rice burrito bowls, or breakfast for dinner.
  3. A one-pot night. One pan to wash. e.g. one-pot chili mac, or pasta e fagioli.
  4. A fun night. The kids help or build their own plate — and are far more likely to eat it. e.g. taco night, or pizza night.
  5. A clean-out night. Late in the week, using up whatever is left in the fridge, so nothing you bought on Sunday gets thrown out on Saturday. e.g. big skillet fried rice.

Put the fast night on your busiest day, the oven night on your easiest, and the clean-out night on Thursday or Friday. That’s the whole method.

Make next week easier than this one

Keep the plan when the week is over. A week that worked is a week you can repeat in a month without thinking, and four weeks that worked is a rotation — at that point “what’s for dinner this week?” is answered before anyone asks. That is the idea the whole weekly template approach rests on.
