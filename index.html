Why meal plans fall apart in week two

Not because parents lack discipline. Because most plans are built for a week that never happens.

Problem one

The deciding is the work

Browning mince takes ten minutes. Choosing what to brown, checking whether you have it, and doing that again tomorrow is what actually empties you out by Thursday.

Problem two

Seven perfect nights

A seven-dinner plan needs seven nights to go right. Real weeks contain a sick kid, a late meeting and a birthday party. PrepSheet plans five and leaves two nights deliberately open.

Problem three

Prep that can’t flex

Five containers of finished meals only pay off if the week goes to plan. One protein, one grain and one vegetable become whatever Tuesday turns out to need.

The Sunday 60

The whole method. Sixty minutes, once a week, at whatever time you choose — Sunday is a suggestion, not a rule.

0–10 min

Pick five dinners

From your Dinner Bank — one page listing the twenty meals your family already eats without complaint. No browsing, no new recipes, no inspiration required.

10–25 min

Write one list

Walk through the five dinners and write every ingredient under its store section. One aisle-sorted list means one trip and no Wednesday dash for a tin of tomatoes.

25–30 min

Post the plan

On the fridge, where everyone can read it. Half the load of dinner is being the only person who knows the answer. A posted plan ends the question.

30–60 min

Prep three components

One protein, one grain, one vegetable. Shred the chicken, cook the big pot of rice, wash and cut the peppers. Components survive a change of plans. Finished meals do not.

Questions

Is this a recipe book?

No. It is a planning system. The four Starter Rotations do tell you what to cook and what to do on the night, but each dinner is described in two or three lines — the kind of note you write for yourself, not a photographed recipe. If you want a cookbook, this is not it. If you already know how to cook twenty things and the problem is deciding and shopping, this is exactly it.

Letter or A4?

Both. Every purchase includes a US Letter file and an A4 file, so it prints correctly whichever side of the Atlantic you are on. Print at 100% / actual size.

Do I need a colour printer?

No. Every page is designed to stay readable in black and white, with thin rules and no heavy ink blocks. We built it that way on purpose — a printable that drains a colour cartridge is a printable you stop printing.

Can I reuse the pages without reprinting?

Yes — slide the Weekly Dinner Plan into a plastic sleeve and use a dry-erase marker. Many people print a stack of ten and keep them in a kitchen drawer instead.

Does it work for vegetarian, gluten-free or allergy households?

The planner pages work for any diet — they are blank. The four Starter Rotations are omnivore and are not built around allergens, so if you avoid gluten, dairy or meat you will be swapping dinners rather than using those four weeks as written. Worth knowing before you buy.
