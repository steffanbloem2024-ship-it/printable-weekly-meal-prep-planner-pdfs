    How the free Week Builder works

    Three steps, about ten minutes, and no account anywhere.

    1. Pick five dinners

    Choose from the twenty family dinners listed below, or type in your own with their ingredients — both end up on the same plan and the same list. Five, not seven: seven-night plans fail in week two, and two open nights are what make the other five survive a sick kid or a late meeting.

    2. The list writes itself

    Every ingredient for your five dinners is merged into one list, sorted into the six aisles of a normal supermarket. Shared items are added up, so two dinners needing cheese become one line with the total, not two lines you have to reconcile in the shop.

    3. Print it for the fridge

    One sheet with the week and the on-the-night instructions, one sheet with the grocery list and the prep block. Half the mental load of dinner is being the only person who knows the answer — a posted plan ends the question.

    Your own dinners work too — that is the point

    No stranger’s list of twenty dinners is your family’s list. So type yours in: a name, and its ingredients separated by commas (“1 lb mince, 2 cans tomatoes, 8 oz cheddar”). Quantities are read if you write them and added to ours, so an ingredient two dinners share becomes one line with the total instead of two lines you reconcile in the shop. Each ingredient is sorted into the aisle it belongs to; if a guess is wrong, start the line with the aisle you want — pantry: rice, soy sauce — and it goes there.

    Dinners you type in are kept under Your dinners and stay in this browser, so next week is picking from a list rather than typing again. They are never uploaded: there is no account here and no server to upload them to. A dinner with no ingredients is still welcome — it goes on the plan and the printed list leaves blank lines for it.
