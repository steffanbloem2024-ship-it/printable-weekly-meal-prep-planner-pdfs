Why this works, and where it stops working

The usual two-week meal plan is really two one-week plans stapled together, and it assumes a second shop you were trying to avoid. The thing that makes one trip work is not planning harder: it is putting the perishable dinners in the first week and the keeping dinners in the second, then buying the shared lines once.

So week one spends the lettuce, the tomatoes, the zucchini, the green beans and the peppers — everything with a short life — while week two is built on tinned beans, lentils, pasta, rice, stock and the four fresh things that genuinely last: onions, carrots, celery and potatoes. Nothing in week two is waiting on an ingredient that will be limp by then.

Being straight with you: A single trip cannot cover everything, and pretending otherwise is how a plan like this fails in the second week. Four things need saying. Freeze on the day you shop: the 1 lb of ground beef for week two’s chili mac and the pack of breakfast sausages, which week one does not reach until Friday — move both to the fridge the night before they are needed. Bread does not last two weeks: the sandwich loaf and the crusty loaf are both week-one lines, and even then the sandwich loaf is for Friday, so freeze it on shopping day and take out what you need. Milk is the one line that cannot be stockpiled — week two’s shepherd’s pie wants a splash for the mash, and most households buy milk again anyway; this plan assumes you will. Fruit for the table is on the list without a quantity on purpose: buy whatever your family eats, and buy it for week one. Of the four keeping vegetables, celery is the shortest-lived — wrap it and keep it cold, and if it has gone bendy by week two it is going into a soup, which forgives it. Everything else on the list — the tins, the dry goods, the cheese, the eggs, the root vegetables — is fine for fourteen days in an ordinary kitchen.

How to run it

  • Shop on a weekend and do the week-one prep block the same day if you can — it is the only prep that happens within a day of the shop, which is why the raw chicken breast gets roasted then. Thursday is four days after that, so the block itself tells you to freeze that portion or roast it on Tuesday instead; take whichever it offers.
  • The second prep block is the following weekend, with no shop in between. That is the one that makes week two feel planned rather than scraped together, and it needs about half an hour.
  • If a night collapses in week one, move Breakfast for Dinner into the gap — eggs, bread and sausages are the most forgiving things on the list. Just put something back in its place on Friday.
  • Week two’s dinners are all swappable with each other, because none of them needs anything fresher than an onion. Week one’s are not: cook them roughly in the order given and the fresh produce gets used while it is good.
  • Leave Saturday and Sunday open in both weeks. Ten planned dinners and four open nights is already a fortnight decided; a fourteen-box plan fails by the second Thursday.
