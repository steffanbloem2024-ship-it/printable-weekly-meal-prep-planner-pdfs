On the night

Monday — Taco Night
Brown the beef with taco seasoning. Put the toppings on the table and let everyone build their own.
Tuesday — Chicken Quesadillas + Tomato Soup
Fill the tortillas with shredded chicken and cheese, crisp in a dry pan. Heat the soup.
Wednesday — Beef & Rice Burrito Bowls
Brown the beef. Build bowls with rice, black beans, corn, cheese and whatever toppings are around.
Thursday — Breakfast for Dinner
Scrambled eggs with peppers and cheese, toast, sausages if you have them, a bowl of fruit on the table.
Friday — Pizza Night
Stretch the dough onto oiled pans, sauce, cheese, bake at 475°F / 245°C for 12–14 min. Kids top their own half.

The prep block — do these once, before the week starts

  • Shred the cheese and lettuce; dice the tomatoes into one container.
  • Roast and shred the chicken breast (oil, salt, 400°F / 205°C, 25 min).
  • Cook the rice.Cooked rice is the strict one: cool it fast, fridge it within the hour, reheat it once. US guidance says three to four days, UK guidance 24 hours — so freeze the later portions on prep day, or cook that night’s rice fresh.
  • Cut the bell peppers.

Prep components, not finished meals: one protein, one grain, one vegetable survives a week that changes.

Why this week

Most “cook with your kids” ideas are weekend projects: biscuits, a cake, an afternoon you do not have on a Tuesday. This week is the opposite. Every dinner is a normal weeknight dinner that takes fifteen to twenty-five minutes on the night, and the child’s job is a piece of it that has to be done anyway — so their help is part of the dinner, not an extra thing you supervise on top of it.

That is also why it looks a lot like our picky-eater week: four of the five dinners are the same. The dinners children will eat are mostly the dinners that are assembled rather than cooked as one thing, and assembling is exactly the part a child can do. If you already use that week, this one swaps the chili mac for burrito bowls and adds a job to every night.

The kids’ job each night

  • Monday, Taco Night: on prep day, tear the lettuce by hand for the toppings container. On the night, spoon the toppings into bowls and put them on the table, then everyone builds their own.
  • Tuesday, Chicken Quesadillas: on prep day, once the roast chicken is cool enough to touch, shred it with two forks. On the night, sprinkle the cheese and chicken over half of each tortilla and fold it. You do the pan.
  • Wednesday, Burrito Bowls: tip the black beans into a sieve and rinse them under the cold tap, then set out the bowl bar and build their own bowl.
  • Thursday, Breakfast for Dinner: crack the eggs into a bowl and whisk them, butter the toast, wash the fruit. You cut the peppers and run the pan.
  • Friday, Pizza Night: spread the sauce, scatter the mozzarella and top their own half. The oven is at 475°F / 245°C, so the trays going in and out are yours.

How to shuffle it

  • Start the week with Taco Night. It is the easiest job on the list and it sets up the idea that kids have a part in dinner here.
  • Keep Pizza Night on Friday. It has the most for a child to do, and Friday is the night with the most patience for flour on the floor.
  • The ground beef is one 3 lb line on the list: 2 lb for the tacos and 1 lb for Wednesday’s bowls. If Wednesday is more than two days after you shop, freeze that pound the day you buy it and move it to the fridge on Tuesday night.
  • The cooked chicken and rice are fine where they are. The chicken is roasted on prep day for Tuesday and the rice is cooked on prep day for Wednesday, both inside the three-to-four days cooked food keeps in the fridge, so nothing here has to be frozen or re-cooked midweek. The prep block does carry one note, about rice: guidance on cooked rice is stricter than for other leftovers, and stricter in the UK than in the US.
  • Leave Saturday and Sunday open on purpose. Five planned dinners and two open nights is the whole method — a seven-box plan fails by Thursday and takes the rest of the week down with it.

Being straight with you: We are not child-development or food-safety experts, and the jobs above are suggestions, not an age chart: you know which of your children can crack an egg and which will throw it. Every job keeps children away from knives, hot pans and the oven, but they still need a grown-up in the room. Wash hands after the eggs and after anything near raw meat; the raw beef and raw chicken are your jobs, not theirs. The pizza dough on the list is bought, not made. The week contains dairy, gluten, egg and meat and is not allergen-aware, so read every line before you shop if you are cooking around an allergy.
