On the night

Monday — Teriyaki Chicken Rice Bowls
Warm shredded chicken in teriyaki sauce. Serve over rice with steamed broccoli and sesame seeds.
Tuesday — Chicken Quesadillas + Tomato Soup
Fill the tortillas with shredded chicken and cheese, crisp in a dry pan. Heat the soup.
Wednesday — Egg Fried Rice
Leftover rice, frozen peas and carrots, three scrambled eggs, soy sauce, a little sesame oil.
Thursday — Pasta e Fagioli
Soften onion, carrot and celery, add crushed tomatoes, white beans, stock and small pasta. Simmer until the pasta is done. Parmesan on top.
Friday — Pizza Night
Stretch the dough onto oiled pans, sauce, cheese, bake at 475°F / 245°C for 12–14 min. Kids top their own half.

The prep block — do these once, before the week starts

  • Roast and shred the chicken breast (oil, salt, 400°F / 205°C, 25 min).
  • Cook the rice.
  • Dice the soup vegetables all at once.

Prep components, not finished meals: one protein, one grain, one vegetable survives a week that changes.

Why this week

Cook-once-eat-twice usually gets sold as a freezer project. This week is the simpler version: the same cooked thing appears twice in the same week, three days apart at most, so nothing needs freezing, labelling or defrosting. You roast chicken breast once and it is Monday’s teriyaki bowls and Tuesday’s quesadillas. You cook rice once and it is under Monday’s bowls and the whole point of Wednesday’s fried rice.

The nights are deliberately in that order. Cooked chicken and cooked rice are good for three to four days in the fridge, so everything that leans on prep day happens by Wednesday, and the two nights that come after it — a one-pot soup and pizza — are cooked fresh from things that keep. It is the one week on this site whose prep block carries no food-safety note, and that is not an accident: the plan was built to sit inside the window instead of warning you about it.

How to shuffle it

  • Roast all 3 lb of chicken breast at once, on two trays rather than one crowded one, and shred it while it is still warm — it pulls apart with two forks and barely at all once it is cold. Keep it in its own juices in the fridge; that is what stops Tuesday’s quesadillas tasting dry.
  • Cook the rice on prep day for Monday, and leave Wednesday’s share alone in the fridge until Wednesday. Fried rice is the one dish that is better for being three days old — fresh rice goes gluey in the pan.
  • Pasta e Fagioli is the Thursday wall night: one pot, no prep-day dependency, and it mostly cooks itself while you deal with homework.
  • If two chicken nights in a row is too much in your house, move Egg Fried Rice to Tuesday and the quesadillas to Wednesday. That keeps both inside the three-day window, which is the only rule this week has.
  • Leave Saturday and Sunday open on purpose. Five planned dinners and two open nights is the whole method — a seven-box plan fails by Thursday and takes the rest of the week down with it.

Being straight with you: This week repeats on purpose, and repetition is the thing families disagree about: chicken lands on two consecutive nights and rice on two of the first three. If that is a problem at your table, this is the wrong week and the no-meat or sheet-pan weeks are better picks. Three pounds of chicken breast is a real roast — about 25 minutes at 400°F / 205°C, longer if the pieces are thick, and crowded trays steam instead of browning. It contains dairy, gluten, egg and soy and is not allergen-aware, so read every line before you shop if you are cooking around an allergy. The pizza dough on the list is bought, not made; making it is a different hour of your life. And the only timing claim we make is the one we can check: cooked chicken and rice keep three to four days in the fridge, which is why nothing here reaches for them later than Wednesday.
