On the night

Monday — Pasta with Hidden-Veg Tomato Sauce + Garlic Bread
Boil the pasta. Warm the blended sauce. Butter and broil the bread.
Tuesday — Chicken Quesadillas + Tomato Soup
Fill the tortillas with shredded chicken and cheese, crisp in a dry pan. Heat the soup.
Wednesday — One-Pot Chili Mac
Brown beef and onion in a big pot, add chili powder, crushed tomatoes, beans, 2 cups stock and the dry macaroni. Simmer covered 12 min. Stir in cheese.
Thursday — Baked Bean & Cheese Burritos
Mash refried beans with cumin, roll with rice and cheese, bake seam-down 400°F / 205°C for 15 min. Freeze the spares.
Friday — Lentil Shepherd’s Pie
Simmer lentils with carrot, onion and tomato paste, top with mashed potato, bake 25 min until the top browns.

Why this week

Batch cooking usually means giving up a Sunday: six hours, forty containers, and a fridge nobody wants to open by Wednesday. This week is the opposite bargain. You cook the dinners you were going to cook anyway, and for two or three of them you make twice as much of the part that takes the time — the sauce, the shredded chicken, the pan of burritos — because the second half costs minutes, not hours.

That is why these five: every one of them has a part that freezes honestly and reheats to something you would still serve. The hidden-veg sauce freezes better than it keeps in the fridge. The shredded chicken for the quesadillas freezes in its juices, which is what stops it drying out, and it is also exactly what our teriyaki bowls want later. The burritos were written to be frozen flat before this page existed. And the shepherd’s pie and the chili mac go in as finished dinners, which is the rarer thing — most batch cooking gives you components, and some nights you want to take a whole dinner out of the freezer and put it in the oven.

How to shuffle it

  • Double the hidden-veg sauce on prep day — it is the single highest-value thing on this page. Freeze half flat in a bag and a future Tuesday becomes boil-pasta-and-done.
  • Roast double the chicken breast for the quesadillas and freeze half shredded, in its juices so it does not dry out. That half is a second quesadilla night, or the teriyaki bowls from Rotation 2.
  • Lentil Shepherd’s Pie and Chili Mac are the two that freeze as whole dinners. Portion them into the containers you will actually reheat from, not one big block you have to defrost in full.
  • Put Baked Bean & Cheese Burritos on your calmest evening. The recipe already makes extra; roll the spares, freeze them wrapped in foil, and they are the fastest dinner in the house for a month.
  • Leave Saturday and Sunday open on purpose. Five planned dinners and two open nights is the whole method — a seven-box plan fails by Thursday and takes the rest of the week down with it.

Being straight with you: Freezing changes texture, and we would rather say where. Cooked pasta frozen in sauce goes soft, so freeze the sauce and boil fresh pasta on the night; the chili mac is the exception only because its macaroni is meant to be soft, and it will be softer still after a freeze. Do not freeze a finished quesadilla either — the crisp is the whole point and it comes back bendy. Freeze the shredded chicken instead and crisp a fresh one, which takes four minutes. The potato-topped pie reheats best from frozen in a low oven; a microwave works but the top stops being a top. Everything here also needs containers or bags and the freezer space to put them in, which is a real cost if your freezer is one drawer under the fridge.
